SF Healthcare Career Ladders

Which entry-level healthcare jobs in San Francisco lead to a living wage

What this dashboard answers

A first healthcare job is a real achievement. The question here is what comes next: which entry roles in San Francisco lead to a job that pays a living wage, and which tend to plateau. Choose a household type at the top right; every page recalculates against that living wage.

  1. The need: who works full time and still earns below a living wage.
  2. Which jobs clear the bar: healthcare occupations that do not require a degree, by SF wage.
  3. Career ladder: where people go when they leave each entry role, priced at SF wages.
  4. Who holds the jobs: the workforce on each rung, by race and sex.
  5. Measuring progress: a living-wage progression metric for a training program.

Who works on each rung

By race, entry-level healthcare work in San Francisco is done disproportionately by Asian and Black workers. The LVN and RN rung looks much more like the city's workforce as a whole. Both are mostly women, as healthcare is generally.

Measuring progress: the living-wage progression rate

Definition. Of participants placed in a cohort, the share whose hourly wage reaches or exceeds the SF living wage by month 24 after placement, reported alongside the share at month 12.

NumeratorParticipants with a verified hourly wage at or above the benchmark at the 12- or 24-month follow-up
DenominatorAll participants placed in the cohort, including those not reached. Unreached participants are reported as their own category, never dropped
WageHourly wage from the follow-up record; if only annual earnings exist, earnings divided by (usual weekly hours × weeks worked)
BenchmarkMIT SF single adult, fixed at the value in effect at placement
Tiers alongsideBelow living wage; single adult; two earners with one child; two earners with two children
DisaggregationRace/ethnicity, sex, entry role, program. Cells under 10 suppressed and noted
Data neededPlacement date, occupation (SOC), hourly wage and hours at placement and each follow-up, follow-up status

Logic model

InputsCoaching staff; employer partners; LVN school partners; tuition and wraparound funds
ActivitiesRecruit working alumni; prerequisite and entrance-exam prep; enrolment support; retention coaching
OutputsParticipants enrolled; prerequisites completed; LVN program admissions
Short-term (0-12 months)Program retention; continued employment during training; wage held or rising
Long-term (24+ months)LVN licensure and placement; living wage at 24 months; progress toward RN

Why federal indicators are not enough on their own

A WIOA performance score is achieved performance divided by a negotiated target, not an outcome rate. San Francisco's PY2023 Adult median-earnings target was $7,600 a quarter; a full quarter at the single-adult living wage is about $16,200 in 2024 dollars. A program can exceed its federal target and still leave its median participant short of what it costs to live here. That reflects the measure, not any program. The living-wage progression rate asks the question the federal measures leave open: not did they find work, but did the work lead somewhere.

User manual

Getting around

  • Pages are the tabs under the title. Each page answers one question, listed on the Overview.
  • Living wage for (top right) sets the benchmark for every page at once. Values are MIT Living Wage for San Francisco County, hourly, per working adult. "Single adult" is the default and the least demanding household type: a wage that misses it misses every single-earner household.
  • Hover (or tap on a phone) any bar, dot or bubble for its exact values and sample details.
  • Table view under each chart shows the same numbers as a table, for screen readers, copying, or checking.
  • Career ladder: click a bubble or use the role menu to see where people in that role go next.
  • The page remembers your theme choice on this device. Links like #ladder open a page directly.

What each measure means

Below living wage (The need)Share of full-time, year-round workers whose hourly wage (annual wages ÷ usual hours × weeks worked) is below the benchmark, deflated to 2024 dollars
Median, 25th, 75th percentileEDD OEWS hourly wage percentiles for the SF-San Mateo-Redwood City area. The 25th percentile is a rough proxy for a new hire's pay
OpeningsEDD projected total openings 2023-2033: growth plus people leaving the occupation or the labour force
Leaves the occupation each yearEDD projected occupational transfers ÷ 10 ÷ 2023 employment
Clinical job above living wageOf people who leave a role (nationally), the share moving to a clinical healthcare occupation whose SF median meets the benchmark. Excludes medical and health services managers
Rung / partial / plateau30%+ / 15-30% / under 15% on the measure above. Analytical cuts, shown so they can be debated

Caveats to keep in mind

  • National moves, local wages. Transition shares are national and from 2002-2015; wages are San Francisco 2026. They describe how occupations connect, not current SF odds.
  • Only people who changed occupation are in the transition shares. Staying put is not counted.
  • Resumes over-represent people with degrees, which probably overstates upward moves for every role. Comparisons between roles are more reliable than any single level.
  • Survey uncertainty. ACS figures carry margins of error; unreliable cells are flagged, not hidden.
  • Public data describes the market, not any program's participants. Participant records are what answer where a program's own alumni go.
